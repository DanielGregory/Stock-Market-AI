Technical Reference

Pipeline Architecture — GRU → SGD → PPO
Stage 1 · GRU Network
2-layer Gated Recurrent Unit that reads 30-day sequences of price/indicator data and learns which temporal patterns precede an up-move over the next 5 trading days. Outputs a probability gru_prob for every row, which feeds into stages 2 and 3 as an additional feature — giving downstream models sequential context they couldn't derive from a single-row snapshot.
Input: 30 × 13 sequence
Output: gru_prob ∈ [0, 1]
Stage 2 · SGD Classifier
Stochastic Gradient Descent linear classifier trained on base features + gru_prob. Uses TimeSeriesSplit cross-validation (no look-ahead leakage) and partial_fit for incremental online learning. Outputs a binary direction and sgd_conf (sigmoid of decision function), which becomes the RL agent's signal of linear-model conviction.
Input: 14 features
Output: prediction {0,1}, sgd_conf ∈ [0,1]
Stage 3 · PPO RL Agent
Proximal Policy Optimization agent that learns when to trust the upstream models. Its observation includes both gru_prob and sgd_conf plus the current position (0 = flat, 1 = long), so it knows whether it already holds. Actions are binary: enter/hold long or exit/stay flat. Steps advance one full week (5 trading days) per decision.
Input: 15 features + position flag
Output: action ∈ {0, 1}

GRU Architecture & Training
Layers
2-layer GRU, hidden size 64
Dropout
0.2 between GRU layers + before FC output
Sequence
30 days of context (≈ 6 trading weeks)
Target
Direction = Close.shift(−5) > Close (weekly binary)
Loss
TradingLoss (see below)
Optimizer
Adam, lr = 0.001, weight_decay = 1e-4 (L2 reg)
Scheduler
ReduceLROnPlateau, patience 5, factor 0.5
Early stop
Patience 7 epochs on validation loss; best weights restored
Epochs
Up to 30 (full run), 10 (demo)
Grad clip
max_norm = 1.0 — prevents exploding gradients in RNNs
TradingLoss = BCE × profit_weight × lag_weight.
Profit weight: |price_change| + 1 — errors on larger moves cost more, aligning training with actual P&L impact.
Anti-lag penalty: 1.5× when the model predicts the same direction as yesterday AND is wrong — pushes the model to anticipate reversals rather than blindly follow lagging indicators like MA and MACD.
Why GRU over LSTM? GRU has fewer parameters (2 gates vs 3) and trains faster with comparable accuracy on shorter sequences. At 30-step windows and this dataset size, the extra capacity of LSTM rarely helps and increases overfitting risk.

partial_fit in batches of 20 — incremental as new data arrives

Why TimeSeriesSplit? Standard k-fold shuffles data, letting future rows appear in training folds. TimeSeriesSplit always trains on the past and validates on the future — matching real deployment conditions.

Prediction flip: if test accuracy ≤ 48%, the model's predictions are inverted before backtesting. A model that's wrong >50% of the time is directionally useful — just backwards.
PPO Reinforcement Learning Environment
Algorithm
Proximal Policy Optimization (stable-baselines3)
Action space
Discrete(2) — 0 = exit/stay flat, 1 = enter/hold long
Obs space
Box(−∞, ∞, shape=(16,)) — 15 RL features + position
Reward
(next_price − entry_price) / entry_price × 100 if long, else 0
Step size
HOLD_PERIOD = 5 trading days (one week per decision)
Timesteps
8 000 (full run), 2 000 (demo)
Seed
42 (random, numpy, torch, SB3) — reproducible runs
Train/test
RL trains on pre-test portion; evaluated on last 100 days
Why PPO? PPO is stable and sample-efficient for discrete action spaces. Its clipped surrogate objective prevents large policy updates that destabilize training — important when reward signals are noisy (stock prices).

Hold-through-up logic: the agent only sells when it chooses action 0 while holding. Consecutive action-1 steps keep the position open, naturally holding through multi-week up trends without churning.

Design Decisions — Interview Q&A
QuestionAnswer
Why weekly (5-day) horizon?RSI-14, MACD 12/26/9, and MA-20 all lag by 9–26 days. Predicting next-day moves asks the model to outrun its own indicators. A weekly window aligns prediction horizon with indicator timescale.
Why a sequential pipeline?Each model adds a signal the next can't compute alone. GRU captures temporal sequence context; SGD adds a linear decision boundary with that context; RL learns meta-strategy — when to trust each.
Why not just use the RL agent?RL alone is sample-starved on short price histories and prone to overfitting noise. Grounding it with GRU and SGD signals pre-filters the search space and stabilises training.
How is leakage prevented?Direction = Close.shift(−5) is computed then the test set (last 100 rows) is held out before any fitting. TimeSeriesSplit ensures CV folds never train on future data.
Why yfinance over Alpaca?yfinance is free with no API key for historical daily data. Alpaca was removed to eliminate dependency on a brokerage account and enable open-source reproducibility.
Why VADER for sentiment?VADER is a rule-based lexicon designed for short financial news text. It runs locally (no API cost per call) and handles negations and intensifiers well for headline-length strings.
